The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

Did any message go out before my legal review of the underlying policy version was actually complete?

policy-04 is my step, and right now se-policy04 is InProgress with VerificationResult PENDING. If policy-07 sends started while my review was still open, then employees received a policy communication that no lawyer had cleared -- and the model would show that in black and white. This is not process pedantry: an unreviewed employment-policy communication can create a binding representation to the workforce, and once it is sent we cannot un-send it. The ordering constraint is the control.

q-counsel-legal-review-gated-send

Did any SMS go out between 20:00 and 08:00 in the recipient's own local time?

comm-sms-policy declares QuietHoursStart 20:00 / QuietHoursEnd 08:00. Quiet-hours restrictions on non-emergency messaging are statutory in several jurisdictions and contractual in others, and a night-time text to an employee about an employment policy change is exactly the fact pattern that generates a complaint. The window wraps midnight, which is where naive implementations quietly get it wrong -- and a witness that is wrong in the safe direction is worse than no witness, because it manufactures false assurance.

q-counsel-quiet-hours-breach

Do we still hold the exact text of every message we sent, for the full 2555-day retention period the policy commits us to?

Both comm-sms-policy and comm-email-policy declare RetentionDays=2555 -- seven years. That is a commitment we made in writing, and in a dispute the question is never 'what did the template say', it is 'what did THIS employee actually receive'. Templates contain variable substitutions; the rendered text is the evidence. If a delivery record exists but its rendered body is empty, we have a receipt with no document behind it, and the adverse inference runs against us.

q-counsel-retention-evidence

Did we send an SMS to any recipient who had not given SMS consent at the moment we sent it?

comm-sms-policy sets ConsentRequired=true. Every SMS to a non-consenting recipient is an independent statutory violation with per-message damages, and the exposure scales with the size of the affected population. Right now the model cannot answer this at all -- there are no per-recipient send records -- so counsel cannot sign off on policy-04, cannot certify to an outside regulator, and cannot even scope the exposure. The absence of the record IS the legal problem: unanswerable and non-compliant look identical from the outside.

q-counsel-sms-without-consent

For every recipient we could not reach, did we correctly refrain from marking their acknowledgement complete?

exc-unreachable is explicit: create an HR case, route to the manager, and do NOT mark acknowledgement complete. A falsely-recorded acknowledgement is the single most dangerous artifact in this entire model -- it is an affirmative written misrepresentation that the employee received and accepted a policy change, and it is exactly the document that gets subpoenaed in a wrongful-termination or unpaid-notice claim. Deemed-acknowledgement by silence is not acknowledgement. I would rather have zero acknowledgements than one fabricated one.
