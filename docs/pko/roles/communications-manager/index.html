The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

Was every template we actually sent approved by me -- the ApprovalRole the policy names -- before it went out?

Both channel policies name communications-manager as ApprovalRole. Right now tmpl-policy-sms.Status says 'Approved' and there is not one byte of data anywhere saying WHO approved it or WHEN. A status string with no approver behind it is not an approval, it is a claim. I need to be able to point at a row and say 'that is my name, that is the timestamp, that is the version of the text I saw'. Otherwise when something goes out wrong, 'approved' means nothing and the accountability lands wherever it lands.

q-comms-deliverability

Did every SMS we actually transmitted carry the approved opt-out text?

comm-sms-policy RequiredContent says 'Short notice, secure link, approved opt-out text' and the approved template ends with 'Reply STOP to opt out of SMS; email notices continue.' The opt-out is not decoration -- it is the mechanism by which consent stays meaningful, and it is the thing that gets clipped first when a message runs long. I want to check the RENDERED body, not the template, because that is what the employee saw. A policy that declares RequiredContent and never verifies it is a policy that documents an intention.

q-comms-optout-present

Did any SMS we sent exceed 160 characters or split into more than 3 segments?

comm-sms-policy sets MaxMessageLength=160 and MaxSegments=3, and I own that policy. An over-length SMS silently splits at the carrier, arrives out of order, and -- this is the part that actually hurts -- the opt-out language is in the LAST segment, so an over-length message can strip the STOP instruction off the end of what people actually read. I have the template body and I have the policy limit sitting in the same rulebook and nothing compares them. This is the most embarrassing category of failure because it was computable from data we already had.

q-comms-oversized-sms

Has any template been edited since I approved it, and did we send the edited version under cover of my old approval?

This is the failure I have actually lived through. A template gets approved, then somebody makes 'a tiny wording fix' to the body, and the Status field still cheerfully says Approved because nothing ever reset it. The approval attaches to a version of the text, not to a row id. If the body changed and the approval did not, then my name is on something I never read -- and given that these are employment-policy communications, that is not a governance abstraction, that is my name on a legal notice I did not review.

q-comms-template-drift

I approved a template and authorized a campaign. Of everybody the pipeline intended to contact, what fraction actually received it -- and where exactly did the rest go?

I signed the approval that let this campaign run and I found out from a witness column that seven intents produced two deliveries. Nobody told me. That is not a delivery statistic, it is a governance failure: the human who authorized a communication has no standing measure of whether the communication occurred. A 29% yield might be entirely correct -- if five of those people genuinely had no lawful channel, refusing was the right call and I want to say so publicly. But I cannot defend a number I cannot decompose. I need yield attributed to a cause, per campaign, computed and not narrated to me after the fact. This is only askable now because SendIntents made the intended population visible; before loop 1 the denominator did not exist anywhere in the model.

q2-comms-approval-to-delivery-yield

For a message we sent last month, can I still show what I approved -- the exact text, the exact approval, on the exact date -- without that answer changing the next time somebody edits the template?

This is the structural problem I most want fixed, and it is mine as much as counsel's. IsDriftedSend recomputes live against the template's current state. That means the historical record of my approvals is not a record; it is a projection of today's configuration onto the past. If I re-approve a template next week to fix a typo, every send from June silently re-derives against the new approval, and my own audit trail changes underneath me. I am the ApprovalRole. My approvals are the load-bearing artifact in this entire pipeline, and they are currently stored in a way that lets a later edit rewrite what I appear to have authorized. Delivery-time truth belongs frozen on the delivery row.

q2-comms-freeze-what-i-approved

One acknowledgement out of six deliveries. Is that because employees ignored us, or because the messages that landed were the ones least likely to be answered -- wrong channel, wrong template, sent into the small hours?

IsAcknowledged=1/6 is the number that decides whether my communications work. Counsel reads it as an obligation; I read it as feedback on my craft, and I need it decomposed or it teaches me nothing. If the five unanswered messages went out on SMS at 03:00 to people whose real channel is email, that is my routing decision and I can fix it next campaign. If they went out perfectly and were ignored, the message itself is wrong and I should rewrite it. Loop 1 gave me the acknowledgement bit, the local send hour, and the channel -- but it never crossed them, because until deliveries existed there was nothing to cross. Cross them and the number becomes actionable instead of merely embarrassing.

q2-comms-message-nobody-answered

When the pipeline refused to send, was it refusing on a rule I set -- or was it overriding an authorization I had already given, for a reason nobody routed back to me?

My approval is an input to a machine. BlockingGateName now tells me which gate stopped each send, and that lets me ask the question that actually matters to an approver: was my authorization respected, superseded, or quietly discarded? A consent-gate refusal is the system working -- I approved a template, the law refused a recipient, correct outcome. A content-gate refusal means the pipeline judged the very text I approved to be unsendable, which is the pipeline telling me my approval was defective and not telling ME. If the machine can invalidate my signature without a notification, then my approval is decorative. I want that condition named as a column so it shows up in a report rather than in a conversation six weeks later.

q2-comms-my-approval-was-consumed-correctly

When we correctly refused to reach somebody, did anyone ever go back and reach them another way -- or does 'correctly refused' quietly mean 'never communicated with again'?

This is the question I want to own, because it is the one nobody else will. Counsel cares whether the refusal was lawful. The pipeline cares whether the gate computed correctly. Both can be satisfied while an employee sits there having received nothing, and by every measure in the model that outcome is a pass. exc-no-sms-consent literally says what to do -- suppress SMS, send approved email instead -- so the correct handling of a refusal is not silence, it is a different send. Loop 1 gave me RefusalCitedAnException at 0/7, which says we have never once done the documented thing. A suppression with no follow-up is not a compliance success; it is an unreached person with better paperwork.
