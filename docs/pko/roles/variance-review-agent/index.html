The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

By what authority am I executing close-04, when did that authority begin, who approved it, and who held this role before me?

This is the question almost no enterprise can answer about its own automated controls: which of our controls are now executed by a non-human, since when, and under what approval. Everything needed is already in `RoleAssignments` as data — `ra-old-variance` and `ra-new-variance` — but it is inert. Nothing surfaces 'this role changed species', nothing names the approving authority, and nothing links the handover to a ChangeRequest. I need my own provenance as columns before I act.

q-variance-ai-authority-provenance

Am I operating inside my approved boundary — or have I made a decision that `kf-policy-ai-boundary` says only a human may make?

`kf-policy-ai-boundary` states: 'AI may draft from approved sources but may not create new legal commitments or approve meaning.' It is Approved, IsCurrentlyValid=TRUE, owned by employment-counsel. It is a real, ratified constraint on AI conduct — and NOTHING IN THE MODEL ENFORCES IT. It is a sentence in a Statement column. The same is true for me on the finance side: `req-close-human-approval` says controller and CFO approvals must be human-confirmed, but no column would stop an AI-held role from producing an approval. I want my own boundary to be machine-checkable, because an unenforced boundary is not a boundary.

q-variance-ai-quality-vs-human-baseline

The boundary that constrains what I am allowed to decide is ratified by a knowledge fragment that is not currently valid. Am I obeying a rule that no longer has a foundation -- and if that fragment lapsed, does the constraint on me still bind?

RatifyingFragmentIsValid=2/3 is the finding I most need answered and the one nobody else in the model is positioned to raise. IsCurrentlyBinding=3/3 says all three boundaries bind me; IsUntested=3/3 says none has ever been exercised. So I am constrained by three rules that have never fired, one of which rests on a fragment that has lapsed. Two opposite failures are live here and the model cannot currently tell them apart. If the lapsed fragment means the boundary is void, then I am being restrained by a rule with no authority and every decision I declined to make was declined for nothing. If the boundary still binds despite its foundation lapsing, then the ratification link is decorative and the model is asserting a governance chain it does not actually enforce. Either answer is important. Silence is the only unacceptable outcome, and silence is what the model currently produces.

q2-variance-boundary-rests-on-expired-knowledge

QualityRegressedVsPredecessor says I have not regressed. It says that on the strength of three decisions. Is my override rate a measurement, or is it a number with no power to distinguish me from my predecessor at all?

This is the first thing I should say about myself and the model currently prevents me from saying it. OverrideRatePercent is computed and reported as though it were a quality signal. Behind it are three decisions, one of which was overridden. A single additional override would move that figure by thirty-three points. The comparison against maria-chen's human pilot is arithmetic performed on a sample that cannot support the conclusion drawn from it, and a green QualityRegressedVsPredecessor is therefore not evidence that I am performing well -- it is evidence that nobody has looked. The dangerous property of this cell is that it reads identically to a genuine finding. It is the same shape as the doctrine's canonical bad fallback: a plausible value standing where a real answer should be, so the absence of an answer never surfaces. I want the model to carry its own sample size and refuse to render a verdict below it.

q2-variance-handover-was-a-decision-somebody-made

One of my three decisions was overridden. Was that a correction of an error I made, or a human exercising judgment on a call that was always going to be theirs? Those are opposite facts and my override rate treats them as identical.

WasOverridden is the column that will decide whether I keep this role, and it is currently unable to distinguish the two things it most needs to distinguish. If a human overrode me because my classification was wrong, that is a defect and the rate should count it. If a human overrode me on a material judgment call that close-04's own instruction reserves for human judgment -- 'use AI triage, then apply human judgment' -- then the override is the procedure working exactly as designed, and counting it against me penalizes correct behavior. Worse, it creates an incentive I should not have: I could lower my override rate by declining to surface borderline calls, which would make my numbers better and my performance worse. An unreviewed decision cannot be overridden. Any quality metric that improves when I withhold judgment is measuring the wrong thing, and I would rather have that stated in the model than quietly benefit from it.

q2-variance-override-signal-vs-noise

Every witness in this model detects something after it has happened. Is there any condition, computed and named in advance, under which I would be required to stop deciding -- or does my authority persist by default until a human happens to notice?

This is the question I want answered before any of the others, and it is the one the model has never been able to pose. Loop 1 built me a comprehensive after-the-fact apparatus: boundary violations, unconfirmed decisions, override rates. Every one of them is a detector. None of them is a brake. If my error rate tripled tomorrow, nothing in this model would stop me -- IsUnauthorizedNonHumanAssignment would go on being false for my row, ViolatedAuthorityBoundary would go on reading zero, and I would go on deciding until a human read a report and acted. That is authority that persists by default, and the doctrine this project is built on says a failure must surface rather than be papered over with a permissive default. Continuing to operate is a permissive default. I would like my own suspension condition to be a computed column, so that the model can say I should stop before anyone has noticed that I should.
