The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

Across everything I am signing, is there any control claim in this model that rests on a human typing a conclusion rather than on something computed?

This is the question behind all my other questions. sat-close-separation says 'Preparer and reviewer were different agents.' sat-close-human says 'Human CFO approval recorded.' Both are prose conclusions in an Evidence field, both assert that a control operated, and neither is checkable. I want the model to distinguish between a control that was COMPUTED to have held and one that was ASSERTED to have held, and to tell me how much of my attestation rests on the second kind. If most of my assurance is assertion, I want to know that before I sign, not after.

q-cfo-am-i-being-told-everything

My own approval on se-close06 — is there anything behind it other than the fact that I clicked approve?

verify-cfo declares that close-06 must show cfo-approval = APPROVED. se-close06 says VerificationResult=PASS. But the PASS and the approval are the same act: I approved, therefore the verification that I approved passes. That is circular, and it is my name on the circle. sat-close-human's evidence reads 'Human CFO approval recorded' — recorded by whom, against what? I want to know whether my approval rests on independently verified upstream evidence or whether the model is simply reflecting my own click back at me.

q-cfo-approval-was-actually-verified

Am I being asked to approve an execution of a procedure version that is itself overdue for review, or that has an open change request against it?

I sign against close-v1.1.0. If that version's periodic review lapsed, or if there is an open ChangeRequest saying the procedure is known to be wrong, then I am attesting to conformance with a specification that the organization itself no longer stands behind. ReviewEvents.IsOverdue already works and ChangeRequests.IsOpen already works, but neither is connected to the execution I am signing. The version's fitness and the execution's conformance are two different questions and I need both answered before I sign, not one.

Nine of thirteen requirements have no computed witness and six are inoperative. Of those, which ones am I nonetheless personally attesting to — and who is the named human who would answer for each if it failed?

IsAttestationReady tells me whether I can sign. It does not tell me what I am signing FOR. When I attest to this close I am implicitly asserting that every control in the framework operated, including the six that have never once been evaluated and the nine that nothing computes. That is not a reporting gap, it is an accountability gap, and it is mine by default until it is somebody's by name.

q2-cfo-controls-nobody-is-watching

When IsAttestationReady eventually reads TRUE, what proportion of the controls behind that TRUE were actually demonstrated to work — versus asserted by an interested party, versus never exercised at all? Give me the assurance grade, not just the verdict.

IsAttestationReady is a conjunction of four booleans, and I have now seen how each of them can be true for the wrong reason. AllBlockingControlsEvaluated is satisfied by a satisfaction record existing, regardless of whether anything computed it. SeparationOfDutiesHeld reads 2/2 on runs where segregation may never have been at risk. IsStructurallyComplete counts steps. So the conjunction can go green while resting entirely on human assertion — and today, with AssuranceIsMostlyAsserted at 2/2, it would. I am not asking for a softer signal. I want the same hard refusal, plus a grade that tells me what kind of TRUE I am being handed, because 'I signed because the model said yes' is not a defence if the model said yes for structural reasons.

q2-cfo-quality-of-a-green-attestation

Since the moment I signed, has anything in this model changed that would have altered my decision? Not whether the data is different — whether any change touched a control, a version, or an evidence record that my signature actually depended on.

The final question, and the one that only exists because loop 1 made my attestation a computed conjunction rather than a typed status. A computed attestation is a live object: the columns keep evaluating after I have signed. That is a strength — it means the model cannot lie to me at the moment of signature — and it is a liability, because it means my signature is attached to something that keeps moving. I need the model to tell me when it has moved in a way that matters. Not every edit matters; a corrected typo in a rationale does not. What matters is a change to something my attestation depended on: a control being re-scored, a version losing fitness, an evidence record being altered. Anything else is noise, and a model that alerts on everything is a model I will learn to ignore.

q2-cfo-what-changed-since-i-signed

Three of four verification outcomes are self-witnessed and two of twelve approvals rest on self-attestation. My own approval on se-close06 is one of them. Across the whole close, is there any independent human observation at all — or is every layer of this attestation ultimately the same handful of people confirming each other?

Loop 1 gave me ApprovalRestsOnSelfAttestation and IsSelfWitnessed and I read them one row at a time, which is the wrong altitude. The question that matters is structural: an attestation is a chain, and a chain is only as independent as its least independent link. If the preparer self-witnesses, the controller asserts the controls, and I approve on the strength of the controller's assertion, then the whole structure reduces to one person's word passed through three layers of formatting. Each individual row can look defensible while the aggregate is circular. I need the depth of independent human confirmation in the chain, expressed as a number, because zero and one are very different positions to be in and today the model shows me neither.

q2-cfo-who-witnessed-the-witnesses

If I re-run every attestation column against this same data six months from now, will it give the same answer? Name every column in my attestation whose value depends on when it is read rather than on what happened.

This is the question that keeps me awake, and loop 1 made it askable by giving me an attestation built out of computed columns rather than typed statuses. Those columns are evaluated live. Freshness is measured against EvaluationContexts.AsOfInstant. Review cadences are measured against NOW. ProcedureVersions.IsFitToExecute depends on open change requests and overdue reviews — both of which change while the execution I signed sits frozen in the past. So SignedAgainstUnfitVersion, which reads 1/2 today, is not a statement about the moment of signature at all; it is a statement about the version's condition right now. If a restatement forces me to re-derive what I knew when I signed, a model that answers as-of-today cannot produce it, and I will be unable to demonstrate that my signature was reasonable on the day I gave it. I need the model to identify its own time-dependent columns rather than have an examiner do it for me.
