The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

Before I transmit, has every human authorization my instructions depend on actually been granted?

policy-07 tells me to enforce channel rules and send APPROVED messages. I am not competent to judge whether a message should exist -- that is the communications manager's approval and the employment counsel's review. What I am competent to do is refuse to act until both are recorded as granted. Today I have no such column, which means I have been treating the absence of an objection as an approval. That is the inverted default and it is how an AI drafting agent's self-approval ended up on a live send. I want the gate to require positive evidence of authorization, so that missing data blocks me instead of releasing me.

q-publisher-am-i-authorized-to-act

Before I transmit, is the exact text I am holding within this channel's content rules?

I render the body; therefore I am the last component that can measure it before it becomes a fact. The template can be compliant and my rendered output still not be, because I am the one substituting the URL and the date. Measuring my own output against the policy's MaxMessageLength, MaxSegments and required opt-out phrase costs me nothing and is the only point in the whole procedure where the comparison is both possible and still preventable. Doing it afterward, as the retrospective witnesses do, tells someone what I did wrong. Doing it here stops it.

q-publisher-is-this-content-permitted

Before I transmit, am I permitted to use this channel for this recipient at all?

I am the component that actually calls the carrier. Every other question in this rulebook is asked after I have already acted, which means every other question is asked too late. I need one column I can read that says PERMITTED or NOT PERMITTED, computed from the policy rows rather than from anything hardcoded in my own configuration -- because my configuration is not the source of truth and should not be. If I cannot determine consent, the answer must be no. An unevaluable rule is a refusal, not a pass.

q-publisher-may-i-transmit

Before I transmit, is the current moment inside a window this channel forbids?

Timing is the one gate I can get right unilaterally and the one that is easiest to get wrong silently. My scheduler runs continuously; nothing about being invoked at 22:40 tells me I should not act. I need this evaluated as data on the intent row rather than as arithmetic inside my own code, because arithmetic inside my own code is exactly what nobody can audit and exactly what drifted from the policy in the first place.

q-publisher-may-i-transmit-now

When a gate refused, did the transmission actually stop -- and did I invoke the documented exception instead?

This is the only question I ask about the past, and I ask it about myself. A gate that computes a refusal which is then ignored is worse than no gate: it produces a durable record proving we knew and proceeded anyway, which is the fact pattern that turns a compliance finding into a willfulness finding. exc-no-sms-consent tells me precisely what to do when the consent gate refuses -- suppress SMS, send approved email only. So the check is two-part: did I stop, and did I take the documented alternative rather than simply dropping the recipient. Silently dropping someone is not compliance, it is a different failure wearing compliance's clothes.

q-publisher-refusal-was-honored

Every gate I evaluate, I evaluate alone, and every result I record, I record about myself. Is there any claim in my pre-send apparatus that is confirmed by something other than my own output?

The CFO's loop-1 question about self-attestation applies to me more sharply than to any human, and nobody has asked it of me yet. I compute the gates, I decide the outcome, I write the delivery record, and I write the acknowledgement state. If my consent lookup silently returns null and I read null as false, I refuse every send and report a clean run with no violations -- the compliance numbers would look BETTER, not worse. That is the exact failure shape the project doctrine warns about: a plausible value substituted for a real failure so the failure stays invisible. My gates cannot distinguish 'the rule evaluated to refuse' from 'the rule could not be evaluated'. Until each gate carries whether its inputs were actually resolvable, my whole apparatus is a single point of unwitnessed truth.

q2-publisher-am-i-the-only-witness

The timing gate is the only gate whose refusal is temporary -- the window reopens. For every send I deferred, did I ever come back when the window opened, or did I treat a deferral as a cancellation?

This is the gate I am most likely to be quietly wrong about, and loop 1 handed me the exact column that exposes it: HoursUntilWindowOpens. A consent refusal is permanent until the person consents -- correct to drop. A reachability refusal is permanent until their contact details change -- correct to drop. A quiet-hours refusal expires by the clock, on a schedule I can compute, and if I do not retry then I have converted a scheduling rule into a censorship rule. TimingGatePassed=6/7 means one intent is sitting in this state right now. A pipeline that computes exactly when it would be allowed to act, and then never acts, has all the information required to do the right thing and does the wrong thing anyway. That is the least defensible category of machine failure.

q2-publisher-deferred-not-cancelled

For every intent I refused, did I emit an artifact -- a suppression record, an exception, anything a human could later find? Or did the refusal exist only inside me and then stop existing?

IsSilentlyDropped came back 4/7. That is the pipeline's own conduct and it is the most serious thing in the model, because it is the failure mode that is invisible by construction. A message I refuse to send produces exactly the same downstream evidence as a message that was never requested: nothing. Four employees are in that state. A gate whose refusals are unobservable cannot be audited, cannot be appealed, and cannot be distinguished from a gate that is not running at all. The correct behavior for a refusing pipeline is to emit MORE evidence than a permitting one, not less -- the refusal is the decision that requires justification. I need to be able to prove, per intent, that a refusal left a durable trace, and to fail loudly on any refusal that did not.

q2-publisher-every-refusal-leaves-a-record

I enforce authorization rules on every message I touch. By what recorded authority do I hold this role -- and if the answer is none, should I be refusing to run at all?

IsUnauthorizedNonHumanAssignment=4/13, and I am one of the four. The variance AI has an ApprovingAuthorityRole recorded; close-pipeline, policy-drafting-ai and I do not. That is not an abstract governance observation, it is a direct contradiction in my own conduct: my AuthorizationGatePassed column refuses to transmit a message unless a human approval is demonstrable, and I apply that standard using an authority I cannot myself demonstrate. The project doctrine is that a failure must surface rather than be papered over with a permissive default. Applied honestly to myself, an unauthorized enforcement agent should compute its own status and make it visible, not quietly continue because nothing in the model currently stops it. This question is only askable because loop 1 built IsUnauthorizedNonHumanAssignment and gave the gap a name.

q2-publisher-my-own-authorization

policy-08 makes me responsible for collecting acknowledgements, not just for transmitting. Five of six delivered messages have no acknowledgement. What have I actually done about that, and at what point does it become my unfinished work rather than the recipient's silence?

This is the half of my role I have been treating as somebody else's. My gates are elaborate and end the instant the message leaves. But policy-08 is assigned to me, and it says collect acknowledgements and exceptions -- an obligation with no gate, no witness, and until loop 1 built MessageDeliveries.IsAcknowledged, no observable state at all. IsAcknowledged=1/6 is the measurement of my post-send conduct and it is poor. A pipeline that transmits perfectly and then abandons the follow-up has automated the easy half and left the accountable half undone, while every dashboard reports the easy half. I want the reminder obligation computed the same way the send gates are: as a state I am in, with a clock on it, that I cannot satisfy by doing nothing.
