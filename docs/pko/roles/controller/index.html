q-controller-execution-diverged-from-spec

Can a procedure reach a publication step from its first step without passing through a human approval gate — is there any route, including alternative and fallback transitions, around the sign-off?

The CFO's sign-off at close-06 is only a control if every route to publication runs through it. IsHumanApprovalGate and GateHeldByHuman say the gate exists and is held by a person; nothing says the step graph forces work through it. An Alternative transition added for an 'immaterial variance' fast path can route straight to publication, and every per-step and per-edge witness stays green. Whether a gate can be bypassed is reachability from the entry step over only the transitions that avoid a gate, at any depth: a closure over a filtered edge set, not a formula.

q2-ctl-asserted-controls-by-who

Both executions diverged from specification. For each divergence, was there a governing instrument behind it — an invoked exception, an approved change request, or a recorded deviation with an owner — or did the run simply do something different and nobody wrote it down?

`DivergedFromSpecification` is 2/2 and that number by itself is useless to me. Divergence is not a violation; procedures meet reality and reality wins. What matters is whether each divergence was governed. Loop 1 built the parts — `RanUnderException` at 1/12, `HasDeviation` at 4/12, `IsUngovernedInvocation`, `ChangeRequests.TouchesLiveVersion` — but never joined divergence to its governing instrument. So today a run that invoked a documented exception and a run that silently skipped a step produce the same red cell. That flattening is exactly backwards: the governed divergence is evidence the control framework works, and the ungoverned one is the only real finding.

q2-ctl-separation-held-or-untested

Of my four requirements that have a computed witness, on how many has that witness ever actually returned a negative? And for the ones where it never has: is that because the control genuinely holds, or because the witness is scoped so narrowly it can never see a violation?

This is the question loop 1 made askable and did not ask. `Requirements.HasComputedWitness` is 4/13 and `IsInoperativeControl` is 6/13, and I spent loop 1 relieved that the four witnessed ones were the important ones. But `NegativeOutcomeCount` is now a column, and I can see that three of those four have never returned anything but Satisfied. A witness with a zero fire count is in exactly one of two states and the model cannot currently tell them apart: either the organisation genuinely never violates that control, or the predicate is mis-scoped and structurally cannot fire. Those two states have opposite remediations — one is evidence, one is a bug — and conflating them is how a control framework rots while every dashboard stays green. I want the model to force the distinction into the open rather than let a zero mean whatever the reader hopes.

q2-ctl-witness-never-fired

For each control that has a witness, is the witness even evaluated at the same steps where the control is bound? req-close-evidence is bound to close-03 and close-08 — is its witness scoped to both, or does it only look at one of them?

The other half of the never-fired ambiguity, and the more insidious one. A witness can be perfectly correct and still never fire because it is scoped to a step where the violation cannot occur. `req-close-evidence` is bound to two steps via sr-c03b and sr-c08. If its witness only ever gets evaluated at close-08 — where the archival actually happens and therefore usually succeeds — then the close-03 exposure, which is where workpapers get left on personal drives, is invisible. Loop 1 gave me binding counts and satisfaction counts as separate numbers. I need them compared per requirement, because a control bound to two steps and evaluated at one is a control with a fifty percent blind spot that reports as fully operational.
