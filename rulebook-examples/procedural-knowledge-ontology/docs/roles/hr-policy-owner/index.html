The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

kf-policy-ai-boundary says AI may draft but may not create legal commitments. policy-03 and policy-06 are both assigned to policy-drafting-agent and se-policy03 was executed by policy-drafting-ai. Is that boundary actually being held?

This is the single commitment in the whole policy procedure that I would be asked about under oath. We wrote down the rule — AI drafts, humans commit — as an Approved Explicit fragment sourced from counsel. Then we built a procedure where an AI executes drafting steps and a pipeline sends the results. Whether the human approval step actually sits between them is a structural property nobody checks. If policy-05 were ever skipped or executed by software, the boundary would be violated and the model would say nothing.

q-hr-ai-authored-commitments

I am the AuthorityRole on cr-policy-delivery and it has been UnderReview with no DecidedAt since 2026-07-19. What decisions are waiting on me personally, and how long have they been waiting?

Being an authority means decisions queue on my name. cr-policy-delivery is the vehicle for closing gap-policy-delivery-receipts on a live policy version — my inaction is load-bearing for someone else's remediation. The model records the AuthorityRole and the RequestedAt and never once resolves the pair into 'Elena owes a decision, and it is N days late'. I would rather the model embarrass me than have the gap stay open.

q-hr-authority-without-review

policy-v1.0.0 was issued 2026-06-01 on knowledge elicited in May from Amina, Noah and me. My stewardship promises a 60-day cadence. Is the policy I own now resting on knowledge older than the policy itself has been reviewed?

Employment policy is where stale knowledge becomes legal exposure — a jurisdictional rule that changed, a consent requirement that moved, a channel constraint that a provider silently altered. My 60-day cadence is the tightest in the model precisely because policy knowledge decays fast. But cadence governs the REVIEW; nothing governs the KNOWLEDGE. A version can be reviewed perfectly on schedule while every fragment underneath it quietly ages past usefulness, and both facts live in this model without ever meeting.

HasDeparted is on RoleAssignments. IsStillEngaged is on Agents and reads TRUE if the agent holds ANY current assignment. So a person can depart the role that made them the authority on a claim while remaining engaged elsewhere, and every loop-1 orphan predicate stays silent. That is precisely the silent-failure shape I care about: the model reports zero orphans because it is asking about the person rather than the role.

Same structural debt the steward raised, seen from the authority's side. The consequence for me personally is that my decision throughput is unmeasurable: there is no column that distinguishes 'I have not decided' from 'I decided and it is done'.

q2-hr-my-approvals-never-read-as-done

Loop 1 confirmed one live decision is waiting on me and that it blocks an open gap. What I want is the full chain from my inbox outward: which knowledge gaps stay open because I have not decided, which steps sit downstream of those gaps, and how many days of that delay are mine personally rather than the process's.

IsLiveDecisionBacklog=1/2 and BlocksAnOpenGap=1/2 tell me a decision is late. They do not tell me what is stuck behind it. A pending CR that blocks nothing is an administrative debt I can carry; one that holds an open gap on a live procedure is me being the bottleneck on someone else's work, and I would rather know that in those terms.
