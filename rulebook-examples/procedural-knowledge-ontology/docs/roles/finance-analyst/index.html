The questions this role asks

Each question is in the role's own voice. The witnessed answer is the substrate's reading of the predicates that were invented to answer it.

Which blocking requirements were attached to a step I ran but never got a satisfaction record at all — not satisfied, not unsatisfied, just never assessed?

This is the failure mode that scares me more than a recorded 'Unsatisfied'. sr-c03b binds req-close-evidence to close-03. I ran close-03 as se-close03. There is no RequirementSatisfactions row for that pairing. The model shows green because absence reads as silence, not as a gap. An unevaluated blocking control is indistinguishable from a passed one, and that is the exact thing SOX testing is designed to catch.

q-analyst-verification-declared-never-performed

Only 5 of 12 executions are `IsClean`. Invert it: of the executions that ARE clean, how many are clean because everything was checked and passed, versus clean because there was almost nothing checking them?

`IsClean` is a conjunction of absences — PASS, no deviation, no unresolved issue, not late. Every one of those is a thing NOT happening. A step with no verifications declared, no blocking requirements bound, and no exception available will read IsClean=TRUE forever, and it will look identical in a report to a step that survived four independent checks. As the preparer I am judged on my clean rate. I want to know which of my clean steps actually earned it, because the ones that did not are where a real problem would hide without ever changing a single column.

q2-fa-clean-but-uncovered

Loop 1 told me `IsBlockingAndUnmet` fired once out of eight, and I took comfort from that. But `Requirements.NegativeOutcomeCount` is now a real column, and six of my thirteen requirements are `IsInoperativeControl`. A control that has never once said no is not evidence that I did the work correctly; it is evidence that nobody has ever tested whether it can say no. I need to know, per step I ran, whether my clearance rests on any control that has demonstrated it can fail. If every control that passed me is mute, my green step is indistinguishable from an unaudited one.

q2-fa-my-own-evidence-quality

Loop 1 told me two of my steps ran on a stale authoritative source. But that answer was computed against eval-current, TODAY. Was the source stale AT THE MOMENT I RAN, or has it merely gone stale since — and are those the same two steps?

This is the deepest defect loop 1 left me with. `OperationalBindings` points at `Steps`, which is the SPECIFICATION, and freshness is measured against `EvaluationContexts.AsOfInstant`. So `RanOnStaleAuthoritativeSource` does not mean 'the data was stale when I certified against it' — it means 'the data is stale now, in July, when someone happens to be looking'. I signed my reconciliation on 2026-07-01. If I re-read that column in December it will say something different from identical underlying facts. For an attestation that is not a nuance, it is the wrong tense entirely. I need a binding observation frozen at run time, on the execution side, that cannot drift after I have signed.

q2-fa-stale-then-vs-stale-now

Nine of thirteen requirements have no computed witness at all. Of the blocking controls attached to the steps I run, how many are in that nine — and is `Requirements.HasComputedWitness` even telling me the truth, given a human typed it in by hand?

Loop 1 gave me `RequirementSatisfactions.IsAssertedOnly`, and it reads 3 of 8. But that whole column rests on `Requirements.HasComputedWitness`, which is a RAW boolean somebody set by hand, and `WitnessFieldName`, which is a free-text string. Nothing checks that the named field exists. If a control is marked as computed-witnessed and the field named does not exist — or exists and is not a witness — then my assurance number is a claim about a claim. We now have `RulebookFields` as a genuine 1096-row catalog of every field in this model. The hand-typed flag should be derived from it, and until it is, I want a column that tells me when the two disagree.
