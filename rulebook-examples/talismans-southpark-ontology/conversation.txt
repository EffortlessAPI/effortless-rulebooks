In logic, open world means one specific thing: a missing fact is unknown, not false. What you're describing is open in the architectural sense: nothing about the set of substrates, tools or authors is assumed complete. But the pun points at something true. The reason the semantic web adopted the open-world assumption was practical: strangers should be able to add to the graph without coordinating, and nobody's silence should count as a denial. The protocol has that same design goal one level up. Transpilers are registered, not bundled; anyone can publish one; a substrate nobody has written yet isn't "unsupported", it's just not there yet.
